An AI chatbot on WhatsApp or your website can answer common questions, qualify leads, book appointments, share order status and reply in your customer's language, around the clock. It should hand over to a person for refunds, complaints, medical or legal questions and anything that needs judgement. For most Indian businesses, the first useful bot automates the five questions your team answers every day.

## What can an AI chatbot handle today?

Modern chatbots are built on large language models from companies such as OpenAI and Anthropic, connected to your own information. Done well, they can:

- **Answer questions** about your services, prices you have published, timings, locations and policies, using your own documents as the source.
- **Qualify leads** by asking what the customer needs, their budget, location and timeline, then passing a clean summary to your sales team.
- **Book appointments** into a calendar or booking system, and send reminders.
- **Share status** of an order, an application or a service request by looking it up in your systems.
- **Reply in Indian languages.** Current models handle Tamil, Kannada, Hindi and others, so the bot can answer in the language the customer writes in.
- **Capture details** into your CRM so nobody types them in again.

The difference between a bot people like and one they avoid is the source material. A bot that answers from your real price list, policies and product data is useful. A bot that improvises is a liability.

## What should a chatbot never do alone?

Draw the line before launch, and build the hand-over into the conversation:

- Approve refunds, discounts or exceptions to policy.
- Give medical, legal or financial advice.
- Handle angry or distressed customers beyond acknowledging them and passing them on.
- Confirm anything your systems cannot confirm, such as stock or delivery dates it cannot look up.

A good bot says "let me connect you with the team" quickly and passes along the conversation so the customer does not have to repeat themselves.

## WhatsApp or website chat?

| | WhatsApp assistant | Website chat |
|---|---|---|
| Where customers find it | In the app they already use | On your website |
| Conversation history | Kept on the customer's phone | Usually lost when they leave |
| Follow-ups and reminders | Yes, with approved templates | Only while they are on the site |
| Setup needs | WhatsApp Business Platform account | A widget on your site |
| Running costs | Meta's per-message charges plus AI usage | AI usage |
| Good for | Bookings, order updates, repeat customers | Pre-sales questions, lead capture |

Many businesses run both from one brain: the same knowledge base and rules power the website widget and the WhatsApp number.

## What does it cost to run?

There are three running costs to plan for:

1. **AI model usage.** Charged by the amount of text processed. For a typical business bot this is modest, and it scales with conversations.
2. **WhatsApp messaging.** Meta charges per delivered template message, with rates set by message category and country, and replies inside the 24-hour customer service window are free. Check the current rates on [Meta's WhatsApp pricing page](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing) before budgeting.
3. **Platform and maintenance.** Hosting, the provider that connects to WhatsApp, and time to review conversations and improve answers.

The build itself is usually a small, fixed-scope project for a single assistant, with larger systems scoped in phases.

## How does a chatbot project start?

The projects that work start narrow:

1. **Pick one job.** List the questions your team answers most often, or the lead details you always chase. That is the first scope.
2. **Gather the source material.** Price lists, policies, FAQs, product data. The bot is only as good as this.
3. **Prototype on your data.** A working version you can test with real questions before anything goes live.
4. **Connect it.** WhatsApp, the website, your CRM or calendar, with clear rules for when to hand over.
5. **Review and tune.** Read real conversations every week at first, fix wrong answers at the source and widen the scope once it is reliable.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do we need the WhatsApp Business API for a chatbot?

Yes, for automation at any scale. The free WhatsApp Business app supports quick replies and greeting messages, but an AI assistant that reads and answers conversations needs the WhatsApp Business Platform, accessed directly or through a provider.

### Will a chatbot replace our customer service staff?

It replaces the repetitive part of the job, not the people. Staff spend less time on the same five questions and more time on the conversations that need them.

### Can the chatbot see our customers' private data?

Only what you connect it to. Give it access to the minimum data a task needs, keep logs, and agree in writing where conversations are stored and processed.

### How long does it take to launch a first assistant?

A focused assistant for one job can be prototyped quickly once the source material is ready. Most of the time goes into gathering accurate answers and testing with real questions.
