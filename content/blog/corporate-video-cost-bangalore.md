A corporate video in Bangalore is priced on what it takes to make, not on how many minutes it runs. Shoot days, crew size, locations, on-screen talent, edit rounds and the number of versions you need set the budget. Published Indian benchmarks put a 3 to 5 minute corporate brand film at roughly ₹3 lakh to ₹15 lakh, according to [IRPR's Indian cost guide](https://irpr.agency/blog/video-production-cost-in-india), which shows how wide the spread is once scope changes.

This guide breaks down where that money goes, so you can brief a production company with a budget that matches what you actually need.

## What sets the cost of a corporate video?

Every quote you receive is some mix of the same building blocks. Knowing them lets you compare quotes line by line instead of by the total.

- **Pre-production.** Concept, script, storyboard, location scouting, casting and scheduling. A testimonial film needs little of this; a scripted brand film needs a lot.
- **Shoot days.** The single biggest driver. Each extra day adds crew, equipment and location costs.
- **Crew size.** A two-person crew can film an interview. A brand film with movement, lighting setups and talent needs a director, camera team, lighting, sound and production support.
- **Locations.** Your own office costs least as a set. Hired studios, permits for public spaces and travel between cities all add up.
- **Talent.** Employees on camera cost nothing extra; professional actors, presenters and voice artists do.
- **Post-production.** Editing, colour grading, sound design, music licensing, motion graphics and captions.
- **Versions.** A main film plus a 30-second cut, vertical reels and subtitled versions is more editing than a single deliverable.
- **Usage.** Music and talent can be licensed for internal use, online use or broadcast, and the price differs.

## A planning table for your budget

Use this to decide where your budget goes before you ask for quotes.

| Element | Lighter version | Heavier version | Effect on budget |
|---|---|---|---|
| Script | Interview led, no script | Fully scripted with storyboard | Moderate |
| Shoot | Half day, one location | Two or more days, several locations | High |
| Crew | Camera operator and sound | Director, camera team, lighting, art | High |
| Talent | Your own team | Professional actors or presenters | Moderate to high |
| Graphics | Titles and lower thirds | Custom animation sequences | Moderate to high |
| Versions | One master film | Master plus social cut-downs and subtitles | Moderate |

A film can be light in one row and heavy in another. A testimonial series with three short interviews and many social cut-downs spends its money in the edit, not on set.

## What do published benchmarks say?

Very few Indian studios publish prices, so treat any benchmark as a range rather than a quote.

- For a 3-minute corporate film shot in Bangalore, [IRPR lists](https://irpr.agency/blog/video-production-cost-in-india) ₹5 lakh to ₹12 lakh, against ₹6 lakh to ₹15 lakh in Mumbai and ₹4 lakh to ₹10 lakh in Chennai.
- For 2 to 4 minute corporate brand films, [IRPR's Pune pricing](https://irpr.agency/blog/video-production-cost-in-pune) lists ₹1.8 lakh to ₹5.5 lakh.
- The same agency notes that a Mumbai crew costs roughly 30 to 40 percent more than a Pune crew, which is a reminder that city and crew rates matter as much as length.
- In the United States, [Vidico estimates](https://vidico.com/news/corporate-video-cost/) $1,000 to $2,000 per minute for basic single-camera work and $3,000 to $5,000 per minute for professional productions.

Per-minute figures are a rough guide at best. A 30-second advert often costs more than a five-minute training video because every second of it is designed, lit and graded with care.

## How do you get more from the same budget?

The surest way to lower the cost per video is to make more from each shoot day.

1. **Plan the cut-downs at the script stage.** If the brand film, three reels and a LinkedIn teaser are planned together, they come out of one production instead of three.
2. **Shoot photography alongside video.** Headshots, team photos and office stills can be captured on the same day with the same lighting.
3. **Film around events you already run.** A conference, launch or award night already has the people, the stage and the energy. Capturing interviews there saves a separate shoot.
4. **Lock the script before the shoot.** Changing the message after filming means reshoots or awkward edits.
5. **Agree the number of revision rounds.** Two clear rounds of feedback keep the edit moving and the cost predictable.

## What should you put in your brief?

A good brief gets you an accurate quote on the first reply. Include:

- **The goal.** Recruiting, a product launch, investor relations, internal training or brand awareness.
- **The audience.** Who should watch it and where they will see it.
- **Length and versions.** The main film plus any cut-downs, vertical formats or language versions.
- **Deadline.** The date it needs to be live, not just delivered.
- **Budget range.** Even a rough range lets a studio suggest the right scale instead of guessing.
- **References.** Two or three videos you like, and one you do not.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a 2 minute corporate video cost?

The length matters less than the scope. A two-minute interview film shot in half a day costs far less than a two-minute scripted film with actors, several locations and animation. Use the planning table above to decide which elements you need, then ask for a quote against that scope.

### Is it cheaper to hire a freelancer?

For a simple shoot, often yes. For multi-camera events, scripted films or work that needs a director, a production company usually delivers more reliably. Our [honest comparison of production companies, freelancers and in-house teams](/blog/video-production-company-vs-freelancer-vs-in-house) covers the trade-offs.

### How long does a corporate video take to make?

Event highlight films can be turned around within days. A scripted brand film needs time for pre-production, the shoot and post-production, usually a few weeks. Agree the schedule before work starts so the deadline drives the plan.

### Do I need to pay for music?

Yes, if the film is published. Licensed music or a commissioned track is part of post-production, and the licence depends on where the film will be shown.
