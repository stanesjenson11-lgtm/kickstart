Good corporate event coverage is planned before the doors open: a shot list built from the run of show, a list of people who must be photographed, a venue check for light and stage positions, and delivery dates agreed in advance. On the day, the crew covers the stage, the room and the moments in between. After it, selected photos and a highlight film arrive on the dates you agreed, ready for LinkedIn, the intranet and the press.

This checklist comes from the events we cover across Bangalore and beyond: conferences, office inaugurations, plant openings, award nights and Diwali parties.

## Before the event

Most coverage problems are planning problems. Settle these at least a week ahead.

- **Share the run of show.** Session times, speaker order, award categories and any surprise moments. The crew positions around it.
- **Name the must-have people.** Leadership, guests of honour, award winners and partners. Share photos or have someone point them out on the day.
- **List the must-have moments.** The lamp lighting, the ribbon cutting, the keynote, the group photo, the toast.
- **Book a venue check.** Stage lighting, LED walls, window light and ceiling height all change what kit the crew brings.
- **Plan the group photo.** Where it happens, who gathers people, and roughly how many will be in it.
- **Set up a headshot corner.** If leadership and speakers need portraits, a small lit corner away from the crowd captures them between sessions.
- **Agree deliverables and dates.** Same-day selects for social, the full gallery and the highlight film each need their own deadline.
- **Check permissions.** Some venues and guests restrict photography. Find out before, not during.

## On the day

A typical corporate event has three things happening at once, and coverage needs to match.

1. **The stage.** Speakers, panels and award presentations, captured from a fixed position with clean audio from the sound desk for video.
2. **The room.** Audience reactions, applause and the scale of the event, which is what makes the stage shots mean something.
3. **The moments in between.** Networking, handshakes, guests at the brand wall, food and decor. These are the photos that get shared most.

For launches and inaugurations, the ceremonial moments matter most and happen fast: the lamp lighting, the ribbon, the commemorative key, the first walk through a new facility. Brief the crew on exactly when they will happen and who will be standing where.

## What to capture by event type

| Event type | Must-have shots | Often missed |
|---|---|---|
| Conference | Keynotes, panels, audience wide shots, delegate questions | Sponsor booths, registration, branded details |
| Launch or inauguration | Lamp lighting, ribbon cutting, leadership with guests | The facility itself, team arrival, signage |
| Award night | Each winner on stage, the trophy handover, table reactions | Winners with their teams after the ceremony |
| Corporate party | Group photos, dance floor, performers | Decor and food before guests arrive |
| Offsite or team day | Group activities, candid team moments | A clean group photo early, before people leave |

## After the event

Coverage only pays off once it is used. Agree these points before the event:

- **Selection.** A curated gallery rather than every frame, edited for colour and exposure.
- **Same-day social.** A handful of strong images or a short vertical clip the evening of the event, while people are still posting about it.
- **Highlight film.** A two to three minute edit for LinkedIn, the intranet and next year's invitations.
- **Usage.** Confirm you can use the photos and video across your channels, in press releases and in recruitment.
- **Archive.** Where the full set lives, and for how long.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many photographers do we need?

One photographer can cover a single-room event with a simple programme. Add a second when a stage programme and networking happen at the same time, and a videographer if you want a highlight film.

### How quickly can we get the photos?

A small set of edited selects can be ready the same evening if it is planned in advance. The full edited gallery and the highlight film take longer; agree the dates before the event.

### Should photography and video be booked together?

It usually saves money and coordination. One crew shares the run of show, the venue check and the lighting, and the photos and film match in look.
