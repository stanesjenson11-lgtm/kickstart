Hire a freelancer for simple, one-camera jobs; build an in-house team when you need video every week; hire a production company for multi-camera events, scripted films and anything where a missed shot cannot be redone. Each option is the right answer for some work and the wrong answer for the rest, and many companies end up using two of them.

We are a production company, so read this knowing that. We have tried to be fair, because the wrong choice costs you more than it costs us.

## How do the three options compare?

| | Freelancer | In-house team | Production company |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost structure | Day rate per job | Salaries, kit and software every month | Project quote |
| Crew size | Usually one or two people | Fixed, small | Scales to the job |
| Equipment | Their own kit | Bought and maintained by you | Full kit, plus hire when needed |
| Multi-camera events | Hard alone | Limited by headcount | Built for it |
| Scripted films | Depends on the person | Possible with the right hires | Core work |
| Speed for small edits | Good if available | Fastest | Good with a retainer |
| Backup if someone is ill | Usually none | Sometimes | Another crew member steps in |
| Knows your brand | Over time | Best | Over time, faster with a retainer |
| Best for | Simple shoots, tight budgets | High weekly volume | Events, brand films, campaigns |

## When is a freelancer the right choice?

A good freelancer is excellent value for a single interview, a product demo on a table, a small internal event or a quick social clip. You talk to the person who shoots and edits, decisions are fast and the day rate is usually lower than a company's.

The limits show up with scale and risk. One person cannot run a stage camera, roam for candids and record clean audio at the same time. If they fall ill on the day of your launch, there is often no backup. Ask about both before you book.

## When does an in-house team make sense?

If your company publishes video several times a week, an in-house team pays for itself. They know your people, your products and your brand, and they can turn around a small edit within hours.

The hidden costs are equipment, software, training and the range of skills one or two people can cover. In-house teams are often strong at fast social content and stretched by a multi-day conference or a scripted brand film. Many companies keep a small internal team for everyday content and bring in a production company for the big moments.

## When should you hire a video production company?

A production company earns its fee when the job is complex or cannot be repeated:

- **Events.** Conferences, launches and award nights need several cameras, audio from the stage and someone capturing the room, all at once.
- **Scripted films.** Brand films and adverts need a director, lighting, art direction, talent and a proper edit and grade.
- **Campaigns.** One production that delivers a hero film, cut-downs, reels and stills needs planning across all of them from the start.
- **Reliability.** A company brings backup crew and kit, and has done the same kind of event before.

## Questions to ask whoever you hire

Whichever route you choose, these questions separate a safe hire from a risky one:

1. **Can I see work like mine?** Not just a showreel, but a full video for a similar company or event.
2. **Who exactly will be on the day?** Names and roles, and what happens if one of them cannot make it.
3. **What is included?** Revision rounds, music licensing, captions, vertical versions and raw footage.
4. **When will I get it?** Agree delivery dates for each version, not just the final film.
5. **Who owns the footage?** Make sure you can reuse it across channels without extra fees.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a production company always more expensive than a freelancer?

Per day, usually yes. Per finished result, not always: a company can capture more in one day with a bigger crew, which can mean fewer shoot days overall and more usable content from each.

### Can we mix an in-house team with a production company?

Yes, and it is common. The in-house team handles everyday content, and the production company handles events and flagship films, often supplying footage the in-house team can keep cutting for months.

### What should a production company deliver beyond the main film?

Agree it up front. Typical extras are social cut-downs, vertical reels, subtitled versions, stills from the shoot and the raw footage for your archive.

### How far ahead should we book a crew for an event?

As early as you can confirm the date. Experienced crews book up around conference and awards season, and early booking also allows a proper venue check.
